# Code review after M10, 2026-10-28

This review covers the whole code base at commit e5d3105, the close of M10. It judges performance, data integrity, the data structures, memory and allocations, the canvas drawing, and the quality of the code. It also records the research sources behind each judgment and the measures that back it.

Six reviewers read one area each. The areas are `Geometry` with `Model`, `InkEngine` with `Export`, and `Persistence`. The other three are the ink and frame loop of `Rendering`, its text, cards, and previews, and the feature packages with `PointerApp`. Each reviewer read the standard and the design documents of its area first. Then it read every source file and reported only findings that the quoted code supports. The reviewers found no high finding. Each finding below has a status:

- Fixed: the fix is in the code, with a test that fails before the fix, or with a measure.
- Verified: a reviewer suspected a fault, and the code or a measure showed none.
- Open: read in the code, not fixed. The section gives the reason and the smallest fix.

Device measures come from the iPad Pro 11-inch (M4), in Release, in the normal state after a restart (D-177 point 4). Mac measures come from an Apple M4 Pro, in release builds. Section 7 holds the results of the checks and the runs.

## 1. Fixed

### 1.1 The final save of the move to the background started before the rows of the ended edit

`AppComposition.enterBackground()` ended the text edit and placed the pending card, then started a task with `saveNow()`. The rows of both travel through the row channel of the canvas in a task of their own. Nothing ordered the two tasks. The save was thus free to run before the rows reached the pending set. The rows then waited for the throttle, outside the extra time of the background. `saveWorkOnScreen()` already waits with `flushRows()` before its save. The final save now does the same. `LibraryBusyTests.finalSaveHoldsSentRows` sends a stroke right before the move to the background and reads it from the file.

### 1.2 A save failure during a card flight did not reach the new canvas

The third failed save sets `isEditingEnabled = false` on the shown canvas only. A child that an open made before the fault became the shown canvas with its old edit state. The parent of a leave did the same. Its pen and eraser stayed on while the alert said that drawing stops, and its rows piled up pending. `finishOpen()` and `finishLeave(into:)` now apply `canEdit` to the canvas that they show. `NavigationTests.saveFailureDuringOpen` fails the saves after the child exists and before the flight ends.

### 1.3 A refused pen stroke stayed in the renderer

The handoff gives the strokes of a pen path to the renderer before the model step (D-122). `AddStroke.apply` throws at the stroke limit after a change. The erase and the undo paths then give the renderer its strokes back, and the pen path did not. The ink stayed on the screen until the next load, and no erase found it. `addPenStrokes` now calls `restoreRenderer(after:)` in its error path. `StrokeLimitTests.refusedStrokeLeavesRenderer` covers it.

### 1.4 A failed cleanup of old tombstones blocked the open of the library

`open()` ran the 30-day cleanup and the incremental vacuum inside the `do` block of the open. On a full disk, the delete of the cleanup returns `SQLITE_FULL`. Next to one damaged page under a tombstone, its query returns `SQLITE_CORRUPT`. Either error aborted the open at every launch, although every live row loads. The cleanup is housekeeping, and the next launch runs it again. It now runs in a `do/catch` of its own, logs its error, and the open goes on with no removed canvases. `RepositoryLaunchTests.failedCleanup` makes the first delete of the cleanup fail with a trigger and reads the live rows after the open.

Source: storage.md section 9 asks the repository to keep the rest of the library readable after a fault. SQLite reports `SQLITE_FULL` and `SQLITE_CORRUPT` per statement. Source: [sqlite.org](https://sqlite.org/rescode.html).

### 1.5 A cut after a pause of the pencil gave a part negative point times

The time of a cut point is the B-spline blend of the four stored times around it. PencilKit keeps a pause of the pencil as a gap in the time offsets. After such a gap, the blend passes the next stored time. The first kept control point after the cut then got a negative offset, and the times of the part went back. `InkPoint.time` is "seconds since the first point" (data-model.md 7.4), and the fault reached the database. Nothing reads the times today, so the fault was latent. `StrokeSplitter.partPoints` now keeps each time at or above the time before it. `StrokeSplitterTests.timesAfterPause` cuts a stroke with a pause of 2 s and checks both parts.

### 1.6 The eviction of stale glyph runs allocated a dictionary on the render thread

`GlyphRunStore.evict` used `Dictionary.filter`, which makes a new dictionary of all kept runs and hashes each of them again. The D-56 text has 9,000 runs. Each eviction thus allocated a few hundred KB inside the 2 ms build budget (PERF-01). The scan now collects the stale keys in a list that stays, and removes them in place.

### 1.7 The curves of two detail runs did not meet when the stride did not divide the run

The shader draws the used points of a run at `start`, `start + stride`, and so on, and always the last point `end`. When the stride does not divide the length of the run, the step before `end` is shorter than the stride. The next run read its neighbor point one full stride before `end`. The two curves thus met at two different points, up to a sixth of a control point step apart. The effect is under a pixel, because a stride above 1 comes only where the points are under a pixel apart. `RunLinker` now gives the next run the real step of the run before it, as the clipped runs of a redraw did already. `DetailRunsTests.neighborOffsets` checks the used neighbor point and that the test stroke has a run with a short step.

### 1.8 A camera flight between zooms a few ULPs apart jumped

`CameraFlight.camera(at:)` divides two differences of reciprocals of the zooms. An ULP is the smallest step between two doubles. For zooms a few ULPs apart, both differences are rounding noise, and the share of the pan jumped between 0, 0.5, and 1. The reviewer measured the worst error of the share on the Mac: 0.5 at 1 ULP, 0.12 at 4 ULPs, 0 at 1,000 ULPs. The fault is rare. A user reaches it only with a pinch within about one part in 10^13 of the target zoom. Two zooms within `CameraFlight.zoomTolerance` (one part in a billion) now count as one zoom. `CameraFlightTests.nearlyEqualZooms` flies between zooms 1, 2, 16, and 1,000 ULPs apart.

### 1.9 Smaller fixes

- The pencil lift started a task with `requestSave()`. The send of the rows at the lift schedules the same save. The task was thus duplicate work and one task creation on the main actor per lift. The lift asks for no save now, and the canvas no longer holds the repository. storage.md section 4 step 5 says so.
- `SlabAllocator.free` did not detect a double free. A second free of a block hands its range to two strokes. A precondition on the used count of the slab guards it now.
- The idle counter of the render thread had a debug assert that a refused sleep reached. If a change arrived, `sleepIfIdle()` refuses the sleep, and the frame of that change can draw nothing. The counter now stays at the sleep threshold in that case.
- The GPU time signpost of each frame built a message with an argument in the completed handler of every command buffer. The handler now builds it only while a tool records (`signposter.isEnabled`).
- `CanvasContent.restore(_:at:ignoringLimit:)` had a parameter that no caller set to `true`, with a comment that named callers that do not exist. The parameter is gone. The parts of an erase go through the batch restore, which has no limit.
- `make perf-compare` reads `.build/Perf/compare-*.xcresult`. The bundles 11 to 20 of an earlier compare with the extra runs of D-177 stayed. The compare of this review thus first mixed 10 new runs with 10 runs of the baseline code and passed. `Scripts/perf-runs.sh` now removes the bundles of an earlier set before the first run.
- Stale comments, each fixed:

  - the long press "on empty canvas for New card (D-08)" (D-117 replaced D-08),

## 2. Verified

Each reviewer listed the invariants that it traced line by line. The list below names the ones that matter most for stored data and for the frame.

- `FractionalIndex`: the byte order equals the alphabet order. `after` and `before` step at the fourth digit with one carry or borrow. The midpoint is the rule of Greenspan, no key ends in `0`, and the loop bound of 66 passes is tight. The tests cover 1,000,000 appends, 100,000 prepends, 1,000 random inserts, and the exhaustion at 64 digits. Figma writes that the index length grows over time, and that normal usage never makes keys of a prohibitive length. The step at the ends of D-182 keeps the keys of one end at 4 digits, as the library of rocicorp does ([github.com](https://github.com/rocicorp/fractional-indexing)). Source: [figma.com](https://www.figma.com/blog/realtime-editing-of-ordered-sequences)
- `HybridClock` is strictly increasing after a step back of the wall clock and at a full counter. It stops at `UUIDVersion7.maxMillis`. `UUIDVersion7.make` sets the version and the variant bits as RFC 9562 asks.
- Each command checks the content before its first write, so a thrown error leaves the content as it was. The round trip of `apply` and `revert` is tested for all eight commands. The history keeps its byte count incrementally and asserts it after each step.
- `SpatialGrid` reports each item once from the cell where its overlap starts. The position table of a crowded cell is built once at 1,025 entries and stays in step with a swap remove.
- The eraser: the capsule distance, the convex hull skip with the phantom points, and the sampling with the bisection that returns the cut on the free side. Also the merge of ranges that touch, the cut rule of D-65, and the drop of short parts. The parallel split assigns IDs in chunk order afterward. A reviewer measured `Eraser.command` on the Mac in a release build. 100 strokes of 300 points take 0.42 to 0.64 ms, with a straight pass and with scrubs of 480 and 960 samples.
- The PDF exporter follows the one-geometry principle: the same B-spline, the same phantom rule, the exact conversion to Bézier segments, and the same text layout and font as the screen.
- `Persistence`: each save is one `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transaction with the `lastTimestamp` row, and the pending set keeps its rows after a failure. Each `sqlite3_*` result code is compared with its expected code. The restore reads its stop flag before each step and rolls back through `sqlite3_backup_finish`. The import checks run in the order of D-162. The statements compile once and are reused. A canvas load is one count and one query per table on the `*_canvas` indexes. The point BLOB is one copy into an uninitialized array. Sources: in WAL mode with `synchronous=NORMAL`, the database stays consistent, and a power failure loses the last commit ([sqlite.org](https://sqlite.org/pragma.html#pragma_synchronous)). STO-06 accepts that. A passive checkpoint stops early next to a reader or a writer ([sqlite.org](https://sqlite.org/wal.html)), which the hard limit of D-147 covers. `integrity_check` finds what `quick_check` skips, in O(N log N) time, which the restore of D-162 pays once per picked file.
- The frame loop: each advance of the frame counter comes with a wait on the ring of three buffers. The eviction of the arena protects the strokes of the last three frames, and the removal of a stroke frees its block three frames later. The five build triggers are exactly `needsBuild`. The redraw clears with a scissored triangle that writes the red channel only. The eraser mask stamps again in the frame of a front flip. The origin minus the camera center is subtracted in `Double` before the `Float` cast. The lock order is always the core lock before the inbox lock, and the completions and the wakes run after the locks. Source: Apple's Metal best practices name a ring of three dynamic buffers as the balance of idle time, memory, and latency ([developer.apple.com](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/3DDrawing/Conceptual/MTLBestPracticesGuide/TripleBuffering.html)).
- The eviction margin of the arena frees about 19 MB of blocks in one call. When a pan crosses the budget, that call comes. The baseline already measures that call: `eviction.slowest.ms` is 0.33 ms on the iPad (`PointArenaPerformanceTests`), well under the 2 ms frame budget, so the sweep needs no cap.
- The stored glyph runs: the layout version, the page generations, and the frames in flight invalidate a run in agreement with each other and with the ring. Every drawn run marks its pages in every frame. The record search of the vertex shader is correct, because record 0 has prefix 0 and no record has count 0.
- The previews: the slots of D-193, the guard that never takes the slot of a shown card, and the `noRoom` set that only a build clears.
- The feature layer: the order of D-122 for an erase and an undo, the pending card of D-123, and the one owner of a `History` at a time. Also the row channel, the restore sequence of storage.md 7.5, and the clock after a restore. No per-sample allocation in Pointer code: the eraser samples, the hover dot, the camera controller, and the drags of a card and a text box post without an array. All stored closures capture `self` weakly, and no task outlives its screen.

## 3. Open findings

### 3.1 Needs a decision of the product owner

1. SAFE-09 says that no unsafe type leaves a `+Unsafe.swift` file. `DrawableSource` and `RenderThread` are `@unchecked Sendable` types whose files hold the main type, and both are parameters or members outside their files. The safety comments are sound. Either the rule text allows a whole type with a safety comment, or the unchecked part moves into a private box, as `InkResources+Shared+Unsafe.swift` does.
2. `CanvasContent` has two parallel paths for the same transitions of the erase records: the single-stroke `remove` and `restore`, and the batch forms with a `ChangeLog`. Four places state the rule that says whether a record is live or a tombstone. A maintainer gains from one path. This is a refactor outside a task (AGENT-02), so it needs a task of its own.
3. A deleted workspace keeps the sessions of its card canvases in `CanvasSessions` until the budget or the limit of 1,000 evicts them. Memory only, up to 64 MiB per canvas inside the 256 MiB budget. The repository knows the tree, so a `removeAll(in:)` needs the child IDs. The product owner decides whether the cost is accepted and documented at M-03, or removed.

### 3.2 Correctness, small fixes with a test

1. `LibraryMaintenance` dooms a stroke by `erased < cutoff`. A re-erase after an undo keeps the first `erased` and updates `modified` only. A stroke erased on day 0, brought back, and erased again on day 35 goes at the launch of day 35 instead of day 65. No user-visible loss, but the change upload of storage.md section 8 loses the re-erase. Fix: `modified < ?1`, as the other tables use.
2. `SnapshotWriter.writeSnapshotNow()` rotates by the file date from the injected clock. If the iPad clock went back past the dates of 7 daily snapshots, the new file is the oldest and goes. The share sheet then gets a deleted file. Fix: pass the new name to `removeOldSnapshots(except:)`, and treat a newest snapshot dated in the future as due.
3. If the stop flag is set and the finish fails, `SQLiteLibraryRepository.restore` throws the error of `backup.finish()` instead of `restoreStopped`. Fix: log the finish error and throw `restoreStopped`.
4. `SQLiteLibraryRepository.create(_:)` upserts. A repeated workspace ID replaces the title, the sort key, and the tombstone flag without an error, against its contract. Fix: a plain `INSERT` for `create`.
5. A load that passes a limit reports `rowsSkipped(count: 1)` whether 1 or 50,000 rows are over it. `workspaces()` never throws `workspaceLimitReached` on a load. Fix: stop the loop on the limit error and report the count from the count query.
6. `open()` awaits the pre-migration snapshot while `connection == nil`. A second `open()` or a `close()` during that await passes its precondition. The app calls `open()` once per composition. Fix: an `isOpening` flag in the preconditions.
7. If a cut lands on an exact integer parameter, `Eraser.changedArea` is one control point short on each side. The effect is a few stale pixels of old ink outside the redraw until the next build. The case needs the transition within a sixteen-thousandth of a segment of a boundary. Fix: round the range as `partPoints` does.
8. `Eraser` and `StrokeHitTest` grow a bounds that already holds half the width by the radius again, and `EraserPath.bounds` already holds the radius. The filter is conservative, so no fault comes from it. But the serial prefilter of M10 task 3.2 passes every candidate that the canvas filtered already. Fix: drop the `expanded(by:)` calls.
9. `PreviewAtlas` frees the earlier slot of a card before it allocates the new one. If no slot can go, the card loses the smaller preview that it had and shows an empty frame until the next build. Fix: allocate first, then free the earlier slot.
10. `PreviewLoader.request` says that it drops a request past its limit. `PreviewAtlas.want` then never asks again. The limits agree today, so no drop happens. Fix: give a dropped request back through the inbox, or make the contract say that the loader never drops.
11. `GlyphAtlas.drop` keeps a copy of the page while it asks the page table to release empty heaps. The 4 MB chunk of the last page of a heap thus stays until the next drop. Fix: end the scope of the copy before the release.
12. `GlyphPageTable` takes a placement index before it makes the buffer, so a failed buffer loses the index. Fix: push the index back in the failure branch.
13. `RenderEngine+ParentImage+Unsafe.swift` rests the safety of the handoff on the 0.35 s of the open animation, because the two renderers have command queues of their own. Fix: a completion flag on the freeze command buffer that `tryGiveParentImage` reads, or a contract line that the caller calls it after the flight.
14. `RenderThread.setDrawableSize` captures `self` strongly in a run loop block, while `wake()` captures it weakly for a stated reason. Fix: `[weak self]`.
15. `leaveByPinch()` lacks the guards of `leave()` on `isLibraryBusy` and `isSwapping`. A cancelled pinch during a restore starts a parent load on a library that is about to be replaced. `closeScreens()` cleans it up. Fix: add the two guards.
16. A finished export presents the share sheet on a controller that left the screen, and UIKit shows nothing. Fix: `guard !Task.isCancelled` before `finishExport`.
17. `Stroke.init` does not check that the points are finite, and `InkPoints.init` neither. The two producers of today check. A third producer hits a precondition of `Rect` or of the grid with a misleading message. Fix: a precondition in `InkPoints.init`, named in the contracts.
18. `Card.init` accepts an empty title, while `retitling` maps an empty title to `nil` and `RenameCard` refuses it. Two forms of "no title" reach the database. Fix: map the empty title to `nil` in `Card.init`.
19. `PresentedHandoffs` keys a handoff by the object identifier of its drawable. An entry whose drawable is never presented stays, and a later drawable at the same address fires the stale handoff. Every attached drawable is presented today. Fix: clear an entry with the same identifier at `attach`.

### 3.3 Performance and memory, measure before a change

1. `GlyphRunStore` builds give each run its page lists through a shared buffer, so every build allocates two small arrays. The builds are bounded by the 2 ms budget. Fix: an inline form of the few page and generation pairs of a run.
2. `TextEncoder+StoredDraw` converts the buffer list to a resource list at each text pass with stored records. Fix: keep a `[any MTLResource]` list in the store.
3. `TextEncoder+Items` looks each item up twice per pass, in `room(for:)` and in `append`. Fix: return the entry from `room(for:)`.
4. The stored text builds use the constant `StoredText.buildBudget` instead of `configuration.buildTimeBudget`. Fix: pass the configured budget at the frame.
5. A full glyph atlas logs one error per glyph per pass, with string interpolation on the render thread. Fix: log once per frame.
6. `InkView.layoutSubviews` posts the viewport at each layout, and a layout with the same size draws and presents a frame. Fix: compare with the last posted viewport.
7. `PointArena` grows with the allocating signposter and an interpolated buffer label. Growth is rare. Fix: `RenderSignpost` and a static label.
8. `StrokeSpline` reserves 8 bytes per segment of the whole stroke per candidate of an erase. A 16,384-point stroke costs 131 KB per erase that touches it. Fix: reserve a small named constant.
9. `StrokeSplitter.makePart` builds the `InkPoints` and the `Stroke` with its bounds before the length test. It also builds a second spline. A part that is too short pays for all of it. Fix: a length check on the control polygon first.
10. `PDFExporter` lays out each text box twice, once for the page bounds and once for the draw, and the bounds pass has no cancel check. For 10,000 boxes that doubles the Core Text work. Fix: one layout per box.
11. `StrokeFactory.makeStrokes` copies each run twice at the pencil lift, up to 512 KB per full stroke. Fix: map the slice.
12. `Stroke.curve` is a public property that only tests read and that allocates a point list. Fix: remove it or mark the exception, as SCOPE-01 asks. `StrokeHitTest.coveredRanges` and `StrokeSplitter.parts` are `public` members of internal enums, which hides them from `make unused`.

### 3.4 Code quality

1. `TextEditing` holds the card move, the menu card, the pending card, and the pending drag, none of which is text editing. Rename it, or move the card state to a holder of its own.
2. The sort key helpers of cards and text boxes, `isAbove`, and the delta math of the three drags each exist twice in `CanvasFeature`. One generic helper over `sortKey` and `id` removes four copies.
3. `FrameRenderer+Edits` and `CommittedLayer+Redraw` each define `union` of two rectangles, and the pattern `dirtyArea = dirtyArea.map { union($0, x) } ?? x` appears six times. `Rect` in `Geometry` gains `union` and `intersection`.
4. The glyph placement loop exists in `writeQuads` and in `placeGlyphs`, and the corner computation in `mapping` and in `appendCard`. A fix in one must be made in the other.
5. The factor 1.5 of the glyph row height in `GlyphAtlas` and the texture limit 16,384 in `InkResources` are literals without a name (STYLE-03).
6. The limit messages of `CanvasStrings` hold the numbers 10,000 and 120 as text, while `WorkspaceStrings` interpolates `Limits`. A change of a limit leaves the canvas message wrong.
7. Raw white colors in `TextEditView` and `PenTool`. The white is a spec value (D-38, D-15), so a token `DesignSystem.Color.ink` keeps the rule and the fact in one place.
8. Several contracts omit a precondition that the body checks: `StrokeSplitter.finish` and `freeRanges`, `StrokeSpline.segments(near:)`, `PDFExporter.pageOrder`, `GlyphAtlas.markUsed`, `GlyphRunStore.location(of:)`, `PreviewAtlas.want`, and `TextEncoder.append`.
9. Six checks are provably true and pad the assertion density of CONTRACT-02: in `StrokeSplitter` (two), `EraserPath`, `StrokeSpline`, `StrokeHitTest`, and `Eraser.split`. In `Eraser.split` the trivial assert is the only check of the function. A precondition on `Limits.maxStrokesAfterErase` there also gives the loop its visible bound.
10. `FrameRenderer` carries about 30 stored properties across 12 files. A split of the ink state from the transition and pinch state pays off at the next feature.
11. `RestoreStop.isStopped` is a getter that changes state for its test countdown. A method name says so.
12. The `.savesFailing` alert and the restore alert present through different paths (`container.present` and `presentOnTop`) without a reason.

## 5. Research sources

The review used these sources. Each line names what the source supports or changes.

- SQLite, "Write-Ahead Logging" ([sqlite.org](https://sqlite.org/wal.html)). The automatic checkpoint at 1,000 pages and the checkpoint starvation by readers. The passive mode stops early next to readers. The checkpoint design of D-146 and D-147 matches it.
- SQLite, "PRAGMA statements" ([sqlite.org](https://sqlite.org/pragma.html)). `synchronous=NORMAL` in WAL mode keeps consistency and loses the last commit at a power failure, which STO-06 accepts. `trusted_schema`, `query_only`, `cell_size_check`, and `mmap_size` are the settings of D-162 and D-166. `integrity_check` is the thorough form, O(N log N).
- SQLite, "Result and error codes" ([sqlite.org](https://sqlite.org/rescode.html)). `SQLITE_FULL` and `SQLITE_CORRUPT` as results of one statement, the basis of section 1.4.
- Figma, "Realtime editing of ordered sequences". Keys are fractions strictly between 0 and 1, and a new key is the average of its neighbors. The key length grows over time. `FractionalIndex` follows it, with the step at the ends of D-182. The library of rocicorp ([github.com](https://github.com/rocicorp/fractional-indexing)) and its Go port show the same step. Source: [figma.com](https://www.figma.com/blog/realtime-editing-of-ordered-sequences)
- Apple, "Metal Best Practices Guide: Triple Buffering" ([developer.apple.com](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/3DDrawing/Conceptual/MTLBestPracticesGuide/TripleBuffering.html)). A ring of three dynamic buffers, which `DrawListRing` and the frames in flight of the arena follow. The same guide asks for one command buffer per frame, which the frame loop does.
- Apple, `os_proc_available_memory` ([developer.apple.com](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/os/os_proc_available_memory)). The value is advisory and changes during the life of the app. The memory tests use it for the growth during one open, not as a budget of the device.
- Swift forums, "Array.removeFirst() is O(n)" ([forums.swift.org](https://forums.swift.org/t/array-removefirst-is-o-n/44430)). The only `removeFirst` on a hot path is `History.perform` at depth 500. It moves at most 20 KB once per pencil lift, so it stays.
- Holzmann, "The Power of 10" ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Power_of_10:_Rules_for_Developing_Safety-Critical_Code)). The source of the rules that the reviewers applied: the bounded loops, the assertions with a recovery, and the checked return values.
